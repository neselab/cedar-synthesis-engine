# CedarBench Benchmark Levels

CedarBench can be used at two difficulty levels. The levels answer different
research questions and should not be collapsed into one score.

## Level 1: Fixed-Target Policy Synthesis

In Level 1, the benchmark gives the model or agent:

- `policy_spec.md`
- `schema.cedarschema`
- `verification_plan.py`
- `references/*.cedar`

The task is to synthesize `candidate.cedar` so that the candidate policy passes
the fixed formal target. The schema and property target are pre-created and
verified with Cedar validation and `cedar symcc`; they may be written manually,
generated by a tool, or produced by an agentic workflow. This level evaluates
policy synthesis against an already formalized target, not schema or target
construction.

## Level 2: Agent-in-the-Loop Intent Formalization

In Level 2, the model or agent starts from natural-language requirements and is
also responsible for constructing the formal target:

1. propose or refine the Cedar schema;
2. propose verifier-facing property atoms;
3. run an AITL/HITL review loop over schema and property atoms;
4. emit `verification_plan.py` and `references/*.cedar`;
5. synthesize and repair the final Cedar policy against that generated target.

This level measures end-to-end intent formalization: schema construction,
schema repair, property atomization, and final policy synthesis. It is the
closer match for organizational policy-authoring claims.

Direct generation often reaches syntactic validity, especially with GPT-5.5 and
an AutoCedar-generated schema, but validation is not the same as approved intent
satisfaction. The ablation study separates the contribution of each layer:
property atoms reduce residual semantic error, native verifier feedback closes
most remaining failures, and the full signal stack closes the hard tail.

## Recommended Reporting

For papers, report both levels:

- **RQ: policy synthesis against a fixed formal target** — use Level 1.
- **RQ: end-to-end agentic policy authoring from natural language** — use Level
  2.

Level 1 is the appropriate setting for ablations because it isolates schema,
property targets, verifier feedback, and signal handling while holding the
formal target fixed. Level 2 is the appropriate setting for case studies because
it measures the cost and value of constructing the formal target itself.
